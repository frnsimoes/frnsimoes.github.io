Queues don’t add capacity. They turn overload into a debt that has to be paid over time.

A synchronous system under pressure has to make a choice: accept the request or reject it. A queue postpones that decision by accepting the work and accumulating it as backlog.

That backlog can only be paid down when capacity exceeds the arrival rate. If a service is scaled for its normal workload, it may have little or no spare capacity to pay down a backlog created by a burst. In the worst case, the system can remain overloaded indefinitely.
